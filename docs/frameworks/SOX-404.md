# AERF × SOX 404

[← Compliance Overview](../COMPLIANCE.md) · [← README](../../README.md)

> **Scope.** AERF is an open wire format for cryptographic receipts of
> AI-agent actions. This page maps AERF v0.1.0-draft.1 to IT general
> controls (ITGCs) under PCAOB Auditing Standard 2201 (AS 2201) for
> Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 attestations. The page is written for
> management, an external auditor, or an implementer evaluating
> AERF for the evidence layer of automated controls in financial
> workflows.

AERF is in scope for the *evidence layer* of automated controls and
ITGCs operating within agent workflows. AERF is out of scope for
control design, financial-statement assertions, and the broader
ICFR program.

## Primary mappings

### ITGC — Audit evidence (PCAOB AS 2201 ¶39)

Source: [PCAOB AS 2201 — An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting Integrated with An Audit of Financial Statements](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)

ITGCs cover the environment in which financial-reporting
applications operate — change management, logical access,
computer operations, and IT security. Where AI agents perform
automated steps within financial workflows (transaction routing,
account reconciliation, journal-entry preparation, etc.), AERF
receipts provide ITGC-grade evidence for those automated steps.

- **Requirement (paraphrased from PCAOB AS 2201 ¶39).** *"When
  an auditor uses information produced by the company as audit
  evidence, the auditor should evaluate whether the information
  is sufficient and appropriate for purposes of the audit by
  performing procedures to test the accuracy and completeness
  of the information and to evaluate whether the information is
  sufficiently precise and detailed for purposes of the audit."*
- **AERF provides.** Each AERF receipt is independently
  verifiable audit evidence: an external auditor (or PCAOB
  inspector) can verify the
  [`signature`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields) with only
  the issuer's public key, separately from the company's
  logging tooling. This addresses the "accuracy and
  completeness" leg of AS 2201 ¶39 for the per-receipt record.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF preserves the integrity of one
  category of audit evidence (per-action receipts).
- **Gap.** Sufficiency, precision, and detail for the broader
  audit objective remain auditor judgement.

### ITGC — Change management

Source: [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)

- **Requirement (typical ITGC).** Changes to automated controls
  in financial systems are authorized, tested, approved, and
  documented.
- **AERF provides.** The
  [`policy_hash`](../../SPEC.md#43-optional-fields) field binds
  each receipt to a specific version of the deployed plan. A
  change to the plan produces a new `policy_hash`. Auditors can
  confirm that, throughout a reporting period, all receipts
  referenced an approved policy hash.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF detects an unauthorized
  policy change at the agent runtime.
- **Gap.** The change-approval process is organizational.

### ITGC — Logical access and segregation of duties

Source: [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)

- **Requirement (typical ITGC).** Access to financial systems
  is appropriately restricted; conflicting duties are
  segregated.
- **AERF provides.**
  [`key_id`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields) (issuer key) and
  the optional
  [`agent_signature`](../../SPEC.md#43-optional-fields) /
  [`agent_key_id`](../../SPEC.md#43-optional-fields) (held
  decision [C-12](../../DECISIONS.md#held-decisions)) capture
  which signing identity attested to the receipt.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF records who attested.
- **Gap.** AERF does not enforce access; that is upstream of
  the receipt layer.

### ITGC — Computer operations

Source: [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)

- **Requirement (typical ITGC).** Scheduled and automated
  processing executes as designed; exceptions are detected and
  resolved.
- **AERF provides.** A per-action receipt stream documenting
  that scheduled or automated agent runs occurred and produced
  the recorded outputs. Chain hashing
  ([SPEC §8](../../SPEC.md#8-hash-chaining-locked-decision-c-5))
  preserves ordering integrity.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF is the evidence stream.
- **Gap.** Operational monitoring tooling (alerting, ticketing,
  exception handling) is upstream of AERF.

## Gaps — SOX 404 / PCAOB AS 2201 elements AERF does not address

- **Control environment, risk assessment, control activities,
  information and communication, monitoring** (COSO components
  beyond the logging slice). Out of scope: COSO components are
  organizational.
- **Financial-statement assertions** (existence, completeness,
  rights and obligations, valuation, presentation). Out of
  scope: AERF preserves evidence about automated control
  execution, not about whether underlying transactions are
  accurate or complete.
- **Management's assessment.** Out of scope: AERF is an
  evidence input; the assessment is management's
  responsibility.
- **Walkthroughs, tests of design, deficiency evaluation.** Out
  of scope: these are auditor procedures; AERF is evidence
  input only.
- **Manual controls.** Out of scope: AERF logs manual
  agent-supervisor actions only if the deployment chooses to
  issue receipts for them.

## Why AERF specifically applies to SOX programs

A common audit issue is the company asserting that its
application logs were not modified during the reporting period.
AERF receipts take that assertion off the table for the actions
they cover: any modification breaks the signature. The auditor
can verify with a single-binary, standard-library tool
independent of the company's infrastructure.

## Security model

The verifier trusts the issuer's public key, distributed out of
band per
[SPEC §9.2](../../SPEC.md#92-public-key-transport-held-decision-c-10).
The issuer's private key is held by the registrant or its
service provider; a key compromise breaks the signature trust
assumption (see [SPEC §12.3](../../SPEC.md#123-key-compromise)).
RFC 3161 trusted timestamps SHOULD be used in the production
profile so an external auditor can confirm receipts existed in
their present form before any compromise window.

## Auditor verification guide

The verifier workflow is identical across frameworks; see the
[AIUC-1 auditor verification guide](AIUC-1.md#auditor-verification-guide)
for exact commands and expected output. The verifier source is
at [`verifiers/go/verify.go`](../../verifiers/go/verify.go);
the canonical example receipt is at
[`verifiers/go/example/receipt.json`](../../verifiers/go/example/receipt.json).
The independent-verifier property matters for SOX attestations
because external auditors do not rely on the audited entity's
log-handling chain.
