# AERF × SOX 404

[← Back to Compliance Overview](../COMPLIANCE.md) · [← Back to README](../../README.md)

Section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act requires management's
assessment of, and external auditor attestation on, internal control
over financial reporting (ICFR). External-auditor work is governed by
[PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201).
AERF is in scope for the *evidence layer* of automated controls and
IT general controls (ITGCs) operating within agent workflows. It is
out of scope for control design, financial-statement assertions, and
the broader ICFR program.

## Primary mappings

### IT General Controls (ITGCs)

ITGCs cover the environment in which financial-reporting applications
operate — change management, logical access, computer operations,
and IT security. Where AI agents perform automated steps within
financial workflows (transaction routing, account reconciliation,
journal-entry preparation, etc.), AERF receipts provide ITGC-grade
evidence for those automated steps.

- **Requirement (paraphrased from PCAOB AS 2201 ¶39).** "When an
  auditor uses information produced by the company as audit evidence,
  the auditor should evaluate whether the information is sufficient
  and appropriate for purposes of the audit by performing procedures
  to test the accuracy and completeness of the information and to
  evaluate whether the information is sufficiently precise and
  detailed for purposes of the audit."
- **AERF provides.** Each AERF receipt is independently verifiable
  audit evidence: an external auditor (or PCAOB inspector) can verify
  the signature with only the issuer's public key, separately from
  the company's logging tooling. This addresses the
  "accuracy and completeness" leg of AS 2201 ¶39 for the per-receipt
  record.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF preserves the integrity of one
  category of audit evidence (per-action receipts). Sufficiency,
  precision, and detail for the broader audit objective remain
  auditor judgement.

### Change management

- **Requirement (typical ITGC).** Changes to automated controls in
  financial systems are authorized, tested, approved, and documented.
- **AERF provides.** The `policy_hash` field binds each receipt to a
  specific version of the deployed plan. A change to the plan
  produces a new `policy_hash`. Auditors can confirm that, throughout
  a reporting period, all receipts referenced an approved policy
  hash.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF detects an unauthorized policy
  change at the agent runtime; the change-approval process is
  organizational.

### Logical access and segregation of duties

- **Requirement (typical ITGC).** Access to financial systems is
  appropriately restricted; conflicting duties are segregated.
- **AERF provides.** `key_id` (issuer key) and the optional
  `agent_signature` / `agent_key_id` (held decision
  [C-12](../../DECISIONS.md#held-decisions)) capture which signing
  identity attested to the receipt. AERF does not enforce access — it
  records who attested.
- **Coverage.** **Partial — evidence only.**

### Computer operations (job scheduling, automated processing)

- **AERF provides.** A per-action receipt stream documenting that
  scheduled / automated agent runs occurred and produced the recorded
  outputs. Chain hashing (SPEC §8) preserves ordering integrity.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** Operational monitoring tooling
  (alerting, ticketing, exception handling) is upstream of AERF.

## Gaps — SOX 404 / PCAOB AS 2201 elements AERF does not address

- **Control environment, risk assessment, control activities,
  information and communication, monitoring** (COSO components beyond
  the logging slice). Out of scope.
- **Financial-statement assertions** (existence, completeness,
  rights and obligations, valuation, presentation). AERF preserves
  evidence about *automated control execution*, not about whether
  underlying transactions are accurate or complete.
- **Management's assessment.** AERF is an evidence input; the
  assessment is management's responsibility.
- **Walkthroughs, tests of design, deficiency evaluation.** Auditor
  procedures; AERF is evidence input only.
- **Manual controls.** AERF logs manual agent-supervisor actions only
  if the deployment chooses to issue receipts for them.

## Why AERF specifically fits SOX programs

A common audit issue is the company asserting that its application
logs were not modified during the reporting period. AERF receipts
take that assertion off the table for the actions they cover: any
modification breaks the signature. The auditor can verify with a
single-binary, standard-library tool independent of the company's
infrastructure.

## Auditor verification guide

See [AIUC-1.md#auditor-verification-guide](AIUC-1.md#auditor-verification-guide).
The independent-verifier property is the value-add for SOX
attestations: external auditors do not rely on the audited entity's
log-handling chain.
