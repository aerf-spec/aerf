# AERF × EU AI Act

[← Compliance Overview](../COMPLIANCE.md) · [← README](../../README.md)

> **Scope.** AERF is an open wire format for cryptographic receipts of
> AI-agent actions. This page maps AERF v0.1.0-draft.1 to EU AI Act
> Articles 12 (record-keeping), 19 (automatically generated logs),
> and 17 (quality management system). The page is written for a
> provider or deployer of high-risk AI systems, a notified body, a
> market-surveillance authority, or an implementer evaluating AERF
> for the technical-recording obligations.

AERF is in scope for the *record-keeping and automatically generated
logs* requirements applicable to high-risk AI systems (Title III).
AERF is out of scope for the policy, documentation,
conformity-assessment, and prohibitive-practices requirements.

## Primary mappings

### Article 12 — Record-keeping (high-risk AI systems)

Source: [Article 12 — Record-keeping (FLI explorer)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/)

- **Requirement (paraphrased; see official text).** *"High-risk AI
  systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of
  events ('logs') over the lifetime of the system. Logging
  capabilities shall enable the recording of events relevant for:
  (a) identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI
  system presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1) or
  in a substantial modification; (b) facilitating post-market
  monitoring referred to in Article 72; and (c) monitoring the
  operation of high-risk AI systems referred to in Article
  26(5)."*
- **AERF provides.** Receipts emitted per agent action over the
  system's lifetime, each carrying
  [`agent`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields),
  [`action`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields),
  [`in_policy`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields),
  [`policy_reason`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields),
  [`observed_at`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields),
  [`policy_hash`](../../SPEC.md#43-optional-fields), and an
  [`evidence_hash_sha512`](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields)
  digest over the inline `evidence` payload (see
  [SPEC §4.2](../../SPEC.md#42-required-fields)). Each receipt is
  Ed25519-signed and chain-linked, addressing the
  technical-recording obligation with cryptographic integrity
  beyond what the article itself requires.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF addresses the
  technical-recording mechanism.
- **Gap.** Article 12 also requires logs to be kept for at least
  six months (paragraph 3 in the consolidated text); retention
  is a deployment responsibility, not an AERF property.
  Field-by-field mappings to Annex III paragraph (1)(a) systems
  (period of use, reference database, input data on match,
  identification of natural persons involved in verification)
  are carried inside `evidence` rather than as first-class
  fields.

### Article 19 — Automatically generated logs (providers and deployers)

Source: [Article 19 — Automatically Generated Logs (FLI explorer)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/19/)

- **Requirement (paraphrased).** *"Providers of high-risk AI
  systems shall keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1),
  automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems, to the
  extent such logs are under their control. Logs shall be kept
  for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the
  high-risk AI system, of at least six months, unless provided
  otherwise in applicable Union or national law, in particular
  Union law on the protection of personal data."*
- **AERF provides.** Per-action signed evidence with independent
  verifiability and chain integrity (see
  [SPEC §8](../../SPEC.md#8-hash-chaining-locked-decision-c-5)).
  Because verification requires only the issuer's public key,
  retained logs preserve evidentiary value even when held outside
  the provider's infrastructure (e.g., in a customer's archival
  store).
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF defines the receipt format.
- **Gap.** The provider remains responsible for the retention
  duration, secure storage, and lawful handling of personal data
  within receipts (see
  [SPEC §4.5 privacy considerations](../../SPEC.md#45-privacy-considerations)
  and held decision
  [C-8](../../DECISIONS.md#held-decisions)).

### Article 17 — Quality management system

Source: [Article 17 — Quality Management System (FLI explorer)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/17/)

- **Requirement (paraphrased).** *"Providers of high-risk AI
  systems shall put a quality management system in place that
  ensures compliance with this Regulation. The system shall
  include, among other elements, techniques, procedures and
  systematic actions for the design, design control and design
  verification of the high-risk AI system, and the systems and
  procedures for data management for the purposes of placing on
  the market or putting into service high-risk AI systems."*
- **AERF provides.** A documented, machine-verifiable evidence
  artifact for runtime decisions taken by the high-risk system.
  AERF is one component of the technical evidence supporting an
  Article 17 QMS — specifically the operational logging element.
- **Coverage.** **Partial.** AERF addresses the operational
  logging element.
- **Gap.** Most of Article 17's elements (design control,
  documentation, data management, post-market monitoring
  procedures) are process and policy controls outside AERF's
  scope.

## Gaps — EU AI Act provisions AERF does not address

- **Title II — Prohibited AI practices.** Out of scope: AERF
  does not enforce or detect prohibited practices; it logs
  whatever the deployment classifies as in-policy or
  out-of-policy.
- **Articles 9–11 — Risk management, data and data governance,
  technical documentation.** Out of scope: programmatic
  controls.
- **Article 13 — Transparency and provision of information to
  deployers.** Out of scope: transparency obligations are
  documentation, not log records.
- **Article 14 — Human oversight.** AERF can record that a human
  reviewer was involved (via `evidence` content or an optional
  `agent_signature`) but does not implement oversight
  mechanisms.
- **Article 15 — Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity.** Out
  of scope: AERF does not perform accuracy or robustness
  testing.
- **Article 16 — Obligations of providers.** Mostly programmatic;
  AERF supports the logging-related obligations in Articles 12
  and 19.
- **Article 26 — Obligations of deployers.** Out of scope for
  most deployer obligations; AERF supports the operational
  monitoring portion.
- **Article 50 — Transparency obligations for providers and
  deployers of certain AI systems.** Out of scope:
  AI-disclosure to users is a UX control.
- **Article 72 — Post-market monitoring.** AERF logs feed
  post-market monitoring but do not constitute the monitoring
  program itself.

## Security model

The verifier trusts the issuer's public key, distributed out of
band per
[SPEC §9.2](../../SPEC.md#92-public-key-transport-held-decision-c-10).
The issuer's private key is held by the provider or deployer of
the high-risk AI system; a key compromise breaks the signature
trust assumption (see
[SPEC §12.3](../../SPEC.md#123-key-compromise)). RFC 3161 trusted
timestamps SHOULD be used in the production profile to bind
receipts to wall-clock time independently of the issuer, which
mitigates back-dating in the event of compromise.

## Auditor verification guide

The verifier workflow is identical across frameworks; see the
[AIUC-1 auditor verification guide](AIUC-1.md#auditor-verification-guide)
for exact commands and expected output. The verifier source is at
[`verifiers/go/verify.go`](../../verifiers/go/verify.go); the
canonical example receipt is at
[`verifiers/go/example/receipt.json`](../../verifiers/go/example/receipt.json).
The independent-verifiability property is particularly relevant
for Article 12: an auditor, notified body, or market-surveillance
authority can verify any sample receipt directly using only the
provider's public key.
